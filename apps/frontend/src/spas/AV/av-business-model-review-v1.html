Slide 03 · Problem / BLAC Map

Which problem earns the right to lead?

Blatant · Latent · Aspirational · Critical — five problems, one wedge

i
Not every real problem is a sellable wedge. Skok's BLAC test asks whether a problem is Blatant (customers know they have it) or Latent (real but normalized), and whether it is Critical (someone is accountable) or merely Aspirational. You lead with Blatant & Critical and sell everything else on top of it. The Canvas maps all five Alpha-Val problems — only one qualifies as the lead.
Problem BLAC verdict Role in the pitch
P-1 · Study burn
FEL studies take 6–12 months; most of it is mechanical, not judgment		 Borderline Blatant / Latent Proof, not lead. Customers have normalized the slowness — pure time-savings risks missionary selling. Show it in the demo.
P-2 · Rationed optionality
Decisions made on 2–3 alternatives when 10–20 should be compared		 Latent / Aspirational The gain multiplier. The cost of the unexplored alternative is never observed — powerful on top of a Blatant wedge, dangerous as the lead.
P-3 · Unprovable numbers
Estimates can't prove where their numbers came from — approval friction, audit exposure		 Blatant & Critical THE wedge — lead here. Disclosure standards and QP review make someone accountable, and the pain has a date: gate review and filing time.
P-4 · Stage restart
Each FEL stage rebuilds from scratch — re-extraction, lost assumptions		 Latent H2/H3 only. Future-tense at point of sale, and lifecycle appetite is unsurveyed. Sell only as "your data isn't trapped here."
P-5 · Procurement blindness
Lead time & sourcing origin drive feasibility more than cost — and are ignored early		 Blatant Differentiator from discovery. Cheap to add (catalog attributes), expensive for competitors to retrofit. Rising with 2026 tariff exposure.
"The trap is leading with P-2 (optionality) because it is the most exciting to demo. It is Latent/Aspirational — the classic B2B danger zone, where nobody is accountable for the cost of the road not taken. Lead instead with P-3, where a named person is held responsible at a gate review. Danilo's tell: provenance must be visible in the deliverable the approver sees, not just in the architecture."
→
The pitch sequence the Canvas implies: lead with P-3 provenance, prove with P-1 speed in the demo, differentiate with P-5 lead-time, expand with P-2 optionality — and hold P-4 lifecycle for the roadmap. This is the inversion of the current website, which leads with speed.

Slide 04 · RSVPed Scorecard

How robust is the model today?

Repeatable · Scalable · Valuable · Disruptive (P) · Defensible

R
Repeatable
Do customers keep buying?
Unproven

Mining capital projects are episodic, not subscription. A single company may run 2–3 FEL cycles per year at most. Repeatability depends on either (a) a large enough customer base to generate consistent ARR, or (b) expanding within accounts across multiple studies. Neither has been tested.

The "Updating" motion (one of the 3Ups) — keeping users engaged between studies — is not yet designed.
S
Scalable
Can it grow without proportional cost?
Plausible

The LLM pipeline architecture is inherently scalable — marginal cost per estimate is compute + API tokens, not headcount. Cost data (InfoMine, CEPCI escalation) can be centralized. The main scalability risk is accuracy: if each new project type requires manual calibration, scale breaks.

Semantic caching and AST-based deterministic sizing (FR-2.6) are the right levers here.
V
Valuable
Significant worth to customer and founder?
Yes — unquantified

The pain is real: manual TIC estimation at FEL-1/2 consumes weeks of senior estimator time and produces outputs that often can't be audited for disclosure. Alpha-Val addresses both. The problem is that the value is asserted everywhere but computed nowhere — the gain/pain ratio is a narrative, not a number.

No validated estimate of hours saved per study or cost avoided per disclosure cycle. This is a credibility gap in every sales conversation.
P
Disruptive (P)
Does it change the rules of the game?
Potentially yes

Making disclosure-grade cost provenance available at FEL-1/2 (not just FEL-3) is genuinely new. Current market: consultants do this manually and charge accordingly. If Alpha-Val can produce auditable FEL-1 estimates in hours, it disrupts the consultant model — which also means consultants are a threat vector if they build competing tools.

The "P" claim depends entirely on accuracy. Matthew's sizing bugs are not a minor UX issue — they undermine the disruption claim itself.
D
Defensible
Is there a moat competitors can't easily cross?
Weak today

At this stage, the defensibility is primarily in the data provenance graph (proprietary cost dataset + citation tracking), the domain-specific LLM fine-tuning potential, and network effects from accumulated project comparables. None of these moats exist yet — they are the result of operating at scale, not preconditions of it.

The real moat candidates are: (1) a proprietary cost database built from customer projects, (2) workflow lock-in through integration with owner's team toolchains, and (3) NI 43-101 / SK 1300 compliance features that are expensive for generalist AI tools to replicate. Lead-time data (surfaced by Danilo) is an uncontested moat candidate today.

No moat without customers. The urgency to close pilots is not just commercial validation — it's moat-building.

Slide 05 · SLIP Analysis

Is the product easy enough to adopt?

Simple · Low Cost · Instant Value · Plays well in ecosystem

i
SLIP is the product-led growth test. Skok's key insight: the value proposition can be highly disruptive, but the adoption experience must be frictionless. If the customer has to change their workflow to use the product, they won't. This is especially important in mining, where teams are conservative and tool fatigue is high.
Alpha-Val today
Gap / concern
S
Simple to use
Upload SOW → get TIC. The core workflow is genuinely simple in concept. The two-pass LLM pipeline hides complexity from the user.
Partially
Accuracy bugs (Matthew's sizing flags) break the "just works" perception. Simplicity is a trust claim, not just a UI claim. One wrong output undoes it.
L
Low initial cost
No pricing defined. The free-to-pro tier model is mentioned but not implemented. Without a trial or freemium entry point, the first commercial conversation starts at a price negotiation.
Missing
No tiered pricing. No self-serve trial. Onboarding friction is high — prospects must engage directly before seeing any value.
I
Instant value
If the accuracy is correct, a user gets a draft TIC with provenance tracking within minutes of uploading an SOW — that is genuinely instant relative to the status quo of weeks.
Strong potential
This is the product's best SLIP dimension. The Aha moment is clear and fast — if the output is trusted. Trust is the gating variable.
P
Plays well in ecosystem
Mining teams use Excel, Word-based SOWs, project management tools, and internal cost databases. Alpha-Val's SOW ingestion is ecosystem-aware, but the TIC output's portability (to Excel, PDF, QP/EPC workflows) is not documented.
Unknown
No confirmed integration with tools teams already use. Lead-time and sourcing data (Danilo's suggestion) could be a connector that increases ecosystem fit significantly.
"The biggest SLIP risk is the 'P' (Plays well). If teams can't export the TIC sheet directly into their existing cost management or reporting workflows, adoption requires a behavior change — which is exactly what Skok warns against. This should be validated in alpha before designing the paid tier."

Slide 06 · Minimum Viable Segment

Who exactly is the first customer?

MVS: a group who all share the exact same need and buy the same way

!
The P1 vs. P2 decision is still the most consequential open question — but the Canvas narrows it. Both customer-discovery interviews pointed owner-side (resource-thin developers running FEL 1–2), and EPCMs are explicitly deprioritized as a poisoned early adopter — Alpha-Val eliminates the billable hours they sell. The remaining question is which persona inside the owner's team leads the pilot: the estimator who uses the tool, or the PM who buys it.
P1 · Independent Estimator / Technical Consultant
The estimator who does this manually today
Pain: Spends 2–4 weeks per study producing a TIC sheet that may not survive a NI 43-101 audit. Has no defensible citation trail.

Buy signal: Pays out of pocket or charges clients for tool access. Decision is individual or small team.

Pricing anchor: Replaces $X of their own billable time. Potentially higher WTP if it expands their capacity.

Risk: Individual estimators may also be the threatened party — if Alpha-Val replaces their core service, they have no incentive to adopt.
Matthew archetype
P2 · Owner's Project Manager / Capital Projects Team
The owner's team running FEL 1–3
Pain: Receives estimates from consultants with no audit trail. Faces disclosure requirements at FEL-3 with gaps in the provenance chain.

Buy signal: Budget already exists for estimating tools and consultant fees. Procurement goes through approval.

Pricing anchor: Replaces consultant fees ($50–200k/study). Much larger ACV potential.

Risk: Longer sales cycle, committee buy-in, IT/security approval. Harder to reach with a two-person startup.
Danilo archetype

The 3Ups — Update, Upgrade, Upsell
  • Update
    Retention: keep users engaged between studies
    Mining projects have episodic FEL cycles. Between studies, what brings estimators back? Candidates: cost index updates (CEPCI/Nelson-Farrar), market alerts for equipment lead times (Danilo's insight), benchmark comparisons. None of these are designed. Without an Update motion, Alpha-Val is a per-project tool, not a subscription.
  • Upgrade
    New capabilities that expand value per user
    HPGR sizing, more process types, Lang/Hand factor automation, mass balance resolution. These are mapped in the PRD. The risk: upgrading before the base product is accurate undermines trust further. Accuracy first, breadth second.
  • Upsell
    Increasing value from P1 → P2 expansion
    The natural upsell is P1 → P2: an estimator who uses Alpha-Val produces a TIC that their owner client can see. The owner then wants the full provenance chain for disclosure, which requires the Enterprise tier. This is a coherent land-and-expand path — but it requires P2 to see the output, which means Alpha-Val needs shareable/exportable outputs now.
Co-creation & Community
Partnership Opportunity — Largely Untouched
Cost data providers, QP networks, cloud platforms
Skok's "whole product" concept: no startup can build everything. Alpha-Val needs: (1) cost data (InfoMine is a start, but is 2015-vintage), (2) equipment supplier lead-time data (uncontested white space per Danilo), (3) QP/CP professional networks for distribution. None of these partnerships are in active pursuit. The Design Partner LOI structure is the right vehicle for starting this — but it needs to be initiated before seed close, not after.

Slide 10 · Honest Risk Register

What could actually kill this?

Risks ordered by kill probability × imminence — not by comfort level

  • P0 · Critical
    Accuracy bugs undermine the entire value proposition
    Matthew's sizing accuracy flags are not a bug report — they are a trust signal failure. Alpha-Val's CORE is provenance and auditability. If the numbers are wrong, provenance is irrelevant. Every external demo and every pitch runs on the implicit claim that the TIC output is correct. This must be resolved before any further external contact, including Jay Roberg (Albemarle, ret., via Matthew) and Ben. The mass balance resolution (FR-2.6) and the sizing accuracy fixes are the same priority as sales. The Canvas names this explicitly as the anti-metric to watch — Matthew found three sizing errors across two test cases, and each one taxes a champion's trust.
  • P0 · Critical
    MVS indecision blocks everything downstream
    Website copy, pitch framing, pricing, pilot success criteria, conference focus, and design partner LOI structure all depend on knowing whether P1 or P2 is the primary persona. Running both in parallel with two alpha testers is not a strategy — it's a pre-decision holding pattern. The cost of indecision compounds weekly.
  • P1 · High
    No validated quantification of value delivered
    The "hours not weeks" claim is the primary commercial hook on the website and in pitches. It is unvalidated. A well-resourced competitor or a skeptical investor will ask: what's the actual time saved per study? What does a false positive in the TIC cost the owner? Without numbers, the gain/pain ratio is a story, not a defensible claim. The Canvas writes the formula — (EPCM cost avoided + engineering hours saved + value of broader optionality) ÷ (price + adoption effort + trust risk), targeting Skok's 10× — but every input is still unmeasured. Alpha testers should be instrumented to capture them.
  • P1 · High
    Consultants as distribution channel are also the threatened party
    If Alpha-Val targets independent estimators (P1), those same people may recognize that the product reduces their billable hours. Unlike TetraScience's hardware problem (the wrong monetization object), this is an adoption incentive inversion. The framing must shift: Alpha-Val expands what P1 can bill — it doesn't replace their judgment, it removes their data assembly labor so they can do more studies.
  • P2 · Medium
    LLM commoditization makes the pipeline a feature, not a moat
    The Lean Canvas is admirably blunt: today, genuinely defensible — very little. The two-pass pipeline is a 6–12 month head start, not a moat; the founder pairing is rare but matchable. The real moat is the benchmark dataset that compounds with every pilot — at 10–15 projects it powers accuracy bands no entrant can match, but that is 12–18 months out and carries an acquisition cost. The implication is sharp: every early pilot is moat-building, not just revenue. Accelerate data collection through early customers.
  • P2 · Medium
    Pitch / product misalignment in external materials
    The website currently leads with Latent/Aspirational pain (speed). The strongest validated pain is Blatant/Critical (auditability/provenance). This is a conversion leak — the people most motivated to buy (those under disclosure pressure) don't immediately see themselves in the pitch. The optionality mock, while high-quality, risks positioning Alpha-Val as a scenario tool rather than a compliance tool. Messaging must be realigned before the next external conversation.

  • Must
    Fix sizing accuracy bugs (Matthew's flags)
    No external demos until this is done. Document the fix with before/after outputs for Matthew to re-validate. Use this as a trust signal when re-engaging.
  • Must
    Make the P1 vs P2 MVS decision
    Run a structured 2-hour decision session using Matthew (P1 signal) and Danilo (P2 signal) feedback. Commit. All subsequent work flows from this decision.
  • High

  • High

  • Plan
